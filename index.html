HOW THIS WAS BUILT

My design, ideated and built in less than a day by Claude Code. I'm not a programmer. I decided what to build, what to cut, and what was fun. The agent did the rest.

1 · THE STARTING IDEA

The inspirations were the board game Forbidden Stars, the original Heroes of Might and Magic Digital Card Game, and Total War Warhammer 3. Both players pick a card in secret, reveal at the same moment, and compare stats in combat lanes. The fight goes three rounds. What I wanted was its scoring: morale wins the game, not bodies. Killing an enemy card is one way to take points off the board, but not the only way to win.

The first version stacked a bluff on top. The enemy would telegraph its next play, and the better you got at reading the tells, the more often it would lie.

2 · CUTTING THE BLUFF

This proved to be not fun. An opponent that gets less honest the better you play just feels like the game cheating. Pulling it revealed that three identical lanes meant your opening move was a fake choice. It gave an illusion of a decision, but wasn't a meaningful choice. Giving each lane its own points bounty fixed it and also emulates historical warfare (hold a strong center, or defeat the center if you can take both flanks).

3 · WHAT THE SIMULATION FOUND

Once it was playable, the agent ran a thousand runs.

Beat the final boss 3–10% of runs Ran out of cards 49–83% of runs

Players were losing nearly every fight and running dry before the end.

4 · TWO FIXES

The warbands at the bottom of the ladder became genuinely weaker, so difficulty ramps instead of starting at the ceiling.

The loss penalty (losing all routed cards) was weakened (lose 1 routed card).

BEFORE AFTER Beat the final boss 3–10% 29–36% Ran out of cards 49–83% 13–34%

5 · MAKING THE FACTIONS READ

Last problem was the enemy. The three warbands had to behave visibly differently or every fight would feel identical. Here's what we came up with.

GOBLINS hunt you down, wherever you are HUMANS form up and concentrate in one lane DWARVES spread out and hold every lane

6 · WHERE IT LANDED

Three fights, then a boss, choosing your road as you go. You get weaker the whole way down, since cards you lose are gone for good, and only the boss gives anything back. A competent player wins about half their fights and finishes the run roughly a third of the time.

Still open: the dwarves have no answer to being overwhelmed in one lane. The bluff could come back now that the fight underneath holds up. And nobody has played this except a simulator, which can tell you what's possible and has no opinion about what's fun.

HOW IT WORKS

A FIGHT

Five lanes, two clashes.
  • Deploy — lay out four units, at most two to a lane. Both lines reveal together and no blows are struck.
  • Clash — commit one reserve, then give every unit its orders and send them in. Twice.
Your opponent commits at the same moment you do. Neither of you can see the other's card until both are down.

EVERY CARD

  • ▲ Strike — what it deals to the lane you aim it at
  • ⬢ Guard — only the frontmost card defends
  • ✦ Morale — what it scores if it survives

THE THREE ARMS

  • Infantry — holds the line. Strikes only from the front rank.
  • Cavalry — strikes only from the front rank, and can CHARGE: ▲+3 ⬢−2 for one clash. Nothing else can.
  • Archers — shoot from any rank, and shoot over the front: they can pick the front or the back rank of whatever they can reach. From the second rank that means their own lane or either neighbour.
A unit behind another is depth, not a queue — but depth is no shelter from archers.

ORDERS

Nothing resolves until you say so. Tap a unit, then tap the enemy it strikes.
  • A front-rank unit facing someone fights the front card of that lane.
  • A front-rank unit facing nothing must wheel into a neighbour.
  • An archer picks a card, not a lane — front or back.
  • Cavalry may charge, at the price of its own Guard.
Units with only one legal target are pointed there for you. Nothing happens until you press CLASH.

DAMAGE

Guard is a wall, not a discount:
  • Strike < Guard → the line holds. Nothing at all.
  • Strike ≥ Guard → the difference comes off their ✦ Morale.
  • ✦ reaches 0 → BREAKS. Off the field, back next fight.
  • Damage ≥ 2× the ✦ it had left → DESTROYED. Gone for the run.
Damage does not carry to the card behind. Excess is spent on the unit in front of it — the only way past a card is through it, or over it with archers.

WINNING

You win on ground, not kills. Each lane's bounty goes to whoever has more units still standing in it. A unit on ✦1 is a whole body holding ground — to take a lane you have to break them off it.

THE RUN

Three rungs then a boss. You choose your road each rung. Lose a fight and your routed cards scatter instead of returning — so a narrow loss costs one card, a collapse costs three. Nothing replaces them until the boss.
